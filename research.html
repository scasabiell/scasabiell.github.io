Research interests

Quantitative ultrasound imaging, wave propagation and inverse problems

My work sits between applied mathematics, physics and medicine. I study how ultrasound waves propagate in layered, heterogeneous media, and how to recover quantitative information about the medium from the measurements.

Quantitative ultrasound imaging in layered media

An ultrasound image shows the structures of a tissue. Quantitative imaging goes further and measures physical properties of the medium, such as the local speed of sound, which can carry information useful for diagnosis, for example in steastosis (also known as non alcoholic fatty liver disease, NAFLD), an ilness that consists in fat accumulation within the liver cells. Since the liver and fat have differents speeds of sounds is interesenting and can be a key for diagnosis to be able to identify the speed of sound. In particular, doing a succesful echography, nowadays, requires that the first layer of fat is lean, if not the images is distorted, (since there is no layer differentation in the echograph); but steastosis, is often seen in pacients with obesity. Which mades the diagnosis even harder with the current ultrasound techniques which give: low levels of sensitivity, specificity, and quantitative accuracy, and results vary among technicians. Early identification of patients may help improve patient outcomes through treatment intervention, including transplantation for those with cirrhosis (advanced state of steastosis). Because of the limitations fo this technique, after the first ultrasound, doctors have to do a biopsy (which is invasive for the patient) to give a diagnosis. So this is another important part of improving nowadays ultrasounds, to prioritice a non-invasive diagnosis.

Wave propagation in heterogeneous media

We consider media where the effective velocity varies slowly on the scale of the wavelength and that contain a very large number of small, randomly distributed inhomogeneities (scatterers). The material parameters are the sum of a function varying on the scale of the medium and a function oscillating rapidly on the microscopic scale of the inhomogeneities. In this regime the scattered field has a specific asymptotic behaviour, which is the starting point of the analysis.

Inverse problems

From the scattered field, we could proof the validity of an estimator of the local map of the effective speed of sound, developed in the Institut Langevin. So far we have studied its resolution, and validate it on synthetic realistic data generated by Émile Parolin.

Research interests

Quantitative ultrasound imaging, wave propagation and inverse problems

My work sits between applied mathematics, physics and medicine. I study how ultrasound waves propagate in layered, heterogeneous media, and how to recover quantitative information about the medium from the measurements.

Quantitative ultrasound imaging in layered media

An ultrasound image shows the structures of a tissue. Quantitative imaging goes further and measures physical properties of the medium, such as the local speed of sound, which can carry information useful for diagnosis.

Wave propagation in heterogeneous media

I consider media where the effective velocity varies slowly on the scale of the wavelength and that contain a very large number of small, randomly distributed inhomogeneities. The material parameters are the sum of a function varying on the scale of the medium and a function oscillating rapidly on the microscopic scale of the inhomogeneities. In this regime the scattered field has a specific asymptotic behaviour, which is the starting point of my analysis.

Inverse problems

From the scattered field, I develop an estimator of the local map of the effective speed of sound. I prove its stability, study its resolution, and validate it on synthetic data generated by Émile Parolin.
